About the science Source, units, and how the sound is made +

The measurement

NASA GISTEMP v4 reports global mean land–ocean temperature anomalies relative to the 1951–1980 average. An anomaly is a difference from that reference, not an absolute temperature. This global dataset does not provide geographic positions.

The local record contains — valid monthly observations. Missing NASA observations are omitted, never filled with zero or interpolated.

NASA GISTEMP source and documentation ↗

The sound mapping

Melody pitch is mapped exponentially from the dataset's minimum-to-maximum anomaly range. Pulse rate and intensity are based on the absolute change from the previous consecutive observed month. Atmosphere timbre follows the standard deviation of up to 12 contiguous observations ending at the selected month. A gap is never bridged; a layer with insufficient prior observations stays silent.

The sound palette and instruments change only synthesis, not the measurement-to-sound mapping. Layers can be enabled, mixed, soloed, muted, previewed, and processed with optional reverb or delay. A preview is an isolated sound-design audition, not a NASA observation or a timeline event. Master dynamics compression limits peaks.

The climate power spectrum uses Welch's averaged periodogram on contiguous monthly anomalies with Hann windows up to 256 months, 50% overlap, and per-window linear detrending. Its units are cycles per year and °C² per (cycle/year), not acoustic frequency.

The NASA Climate Spiral video is not used as a synchronized timeline. No frame-to-month relationship is assumed.

MATHEMATICAL DATA-TO-SOUND SPECIFICATION

How Data Controls Sound

Musical Layer Measured Variable Scientific Unit Mathematical Translation Audible Result
Melody (Pitch) Global Surface Temperature Anomaly Degrees Celsius (°C) f = 220 × 2^(norm × 1.35) Higher positive anomalies produce higher musical pitches. A -0.81°C anomaly sounds at ~220 Hz (A3); a +1.55°C anomaly sounds at ~560 Hz (C#5).
Pulse (Rhythm) Absolute Consecutive-Month Difference |ΔT| in °C Rate = 0.5 + 5.5 × (|ΔT| / max|ΔT|) Large month-to-month swings generate fast, urgent rhythmic beats (up to 6 Hz); steady consecutive months sound quiet ticks or silence.
Atmosphere (Timbre) 12-Month Rolling Standard Deviation σ₁₂ in °C Cutoff = 350 + 3650 × σ_norm Volatile periods open the low-pass filter (up to 4 kHz) adding rich harmonic brightness; climate equilibrium yields a pure, muted tone.

🔬 Verified Scientific Measurements

  • Empirical Temperature Anomalies: NASA GISTEMP v4 land-ocean temperature data from 1880 to 2026.
  • Climatological Reference Period: Fixed 1951–1980 mean temperature baseline.
  • Chronological Precision: Every data note corresponds to one specific historical calendar month.
  • No Fabricated Data: Missing observations (e.g. future months) are never interpolated as zeros.

🎨 Creative & Artistic Choices

  • Base Pitch Selection: Root frequency set to 220 Hz (A3) for warm acoustic resonance.
  • Synthesizer Waveforms: Harmonic partials (triangles, sines, saws) crafted for aesthetic appeal.
  • Spatial Effects: Reverb room acoustics and stereo ping-pong delay to create listening immersion.
  • Playback Interval: User-adjustable observation speed (0.5× to 4×) to allow broad pattern recognition.
