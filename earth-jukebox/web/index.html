About the science Source, units, and how the sound is made +

The measurement

NASA GISTEMP v4 reports global mean land–ocean temperature anomalies relative to the 1951–1980 average. An anomaly is a difference from that reference, not an absolute temperature. This global dataset does not provide geographic positions.

The local record contains — valid monthly observations. Missing NASA observations are omitted, never filled with zero or interpolated.

NASA GISTEMP source and documentation ↗

The sound mapping

Melody pitch is mapped exponentially from the dataset's minimum-to-maximum anomaly range. Pulse rate and intensity are based on the absolute change from the previous consecutive observed month. Atmosphere timbre follows the standard deviation of up to 12 contiguous observations ending at the selected month. A gap is never bridged; a layer with insufficient prior observations stays silent.

The sound palette and instruments change only synthesis, not the measurement-to-sound mapping. Layers can be enabled, mixed, soloed, muted, previewed, and processed with optional reverb or delay. A preview is an isolated sound-design audition, not a NASA observation or a timeline event. Master dynamics compression limits peaks.

The climate power spectrum uses Welch's averaged periodogram on contiguous monthly anomalies with Hann windows up to 256 months, 50% overlap, and per-window linear detrending. Its units are cycles per year and °C² per (cycle/year), not acoustic frequency.

The NASA Climate Spiral video is not used as a synchronized timeline. No frame-to-month relationship is assumed.
